About

I'm a PhD student in Information Science at Peking University and the Research Center for Digital Humanities, supervised by Prof. Jun Wang and advised on research by Prof. Qi Su. My research focuses on AI for humanities research: building language models and agents that help scholars work with primary sources, connect evidence, and develop interpretations.

My work brings together research agents and retrieval, knowledge graphs and cultural computing, and interpretable multimodal AI. I collaborate with domain experts to translate scholarly and clinical knowledge into annotation protocols, computational representations, and evaluation criteria. Current projects span evidence-grounded scholarship, historical idea modeling, Buddhist authorship attribution, and LLM values and stances.

Previously, I received my M.A. in Computational Linguistics and Language Technology, with a minor in Informatics, from the University of Zurich. My master's research was supervised by Prof. Janna Hastings and focused on interpretable vision-language models for retinal diagnosis. I also worked with Prof. Fabrizio Gilardi on the infrastructure for LLM-assisted online deliberation. Before computational linguistics, I studied Hindi language and literature.
